Shuheng Zhang 章书恒

I am a PhD student in Mechanical and Aerospace Engineering (MAE) at UC San Diego. I was trained as a mathematician, and my research now lies at the intersection of mathematics, control, artificial intelligence, and robotics — on how intelligent systems learn, act, and organize themselves.

Before joining UC San Diego, I received my BSc in mathematics from the Chinese University of Hong Kong (Shenzhen) and an MSc with Distinction in mathematics from University of Oxford.

Outside research, I am building a campervan, making films, taking photographs, and writing. Mathematics, science, and cinema are different languages for uncovering hidden structures in the world and making them visible.

Projects

I was trained in mathematics and now work between control, artificial intelligence, and robotics, on how intelligent systems learn, act, and organize themselves. Four threads run through it: control of infinite-dimensional systems such as PDEs; how a learning system can choose for itself what to study next; robots whose behaviour follows their own survival rather than a human task; and one architecture that holds network science, language models, reinforcement learning, motion planning, and classical control together instead of treating them as separate tools. Three projects carry this into practice and run live on this site: a language agent that has to work out how to walk a body it was told nothing about, an autonomous investment research society, and a conversation system that listens for where research comes from.

Hexapod — Survival Is the Only TaskOpen console →

Embodied-agent experiment · 2026 · console at hexapod.shzhang.com (login required)

A language agent is sealed in a container and handed a body it knows nothing about — a simulated hexapod reachable only over HTTP — with a one-page datasheet and a single instruction: stay alive. That it has six legs, how to stand, how to walk, and that fruit stops the battery draining are all things it must find out by experiment. There is no training. Its workspace persists after it dies, so each new instance inherits whatever the last one wrote down; the question is whether experience compiles into files.

Longview — An Autonomous Investment Research SocietyOpen site →

Multi-agent research system · 2026 – present · live at longview.shzhang.com (login required)

Four persistent identities — an actor, a critic, a steward and a doctor — share one world graph and are built to disagree, preserving the disagreement rather than resolving it. They read primary filings rather than prices, and every claim enters the graph as an edge with the document behind it: a thesis is admitted only with the fact that supports it and the filing that would falsify it. The horizon is deliberately long; its only edge is time, not speed. Nothing here is advice, and the system never trades.

Predictor Feedback for an Age-Structured Population Model with Input Delay

With Mamadou Diagne and Miroslav Krstic · UC San Diego · Accepted, 2026 IEEE Conference on Decision and Control

Stabilization of a nonlinear age-structured population model whose control input — the dilution rate — reaches the plant only after a pure delay. A normalized spectral functional splits the PDE into a scalar amplitude ODE, the only place the delay enters, and an autonomous integral delay equation for the shape, which decays on its own. That cascade admits a predictor state feedback; PDE backstepping and Lyapunov analysis then give exponential stability of the closed loop, with a decay rate explicit in the actuator delay.

Random Vortex Method for 2D Incompressible Flows

M.Sc. dissertation · University of Oxford · 2025
Supervised by Prof. Zhongmin Qian

A probabilistic numerical scheme for the two-dimensional Navier–Stokes equations: vorticity is carried by random vortex particles whose stochastic trajectories reproduce the viscous flow, and velocity is recovered through the Biot–Savart law. The dissertation develops the method on the unbounded plane and then on domains with no-slip boundaries — a half-plane, a corner, and an infinite belt.Read paper →

Hierarchical Feature-Based Synthesis of a Brain Module

Network science research project · University of Oxford · 2025

A generative pipeline for synthetic brain networks built on the Drosophila connectome. Flow-based community detection, Hodge decomposition, PageRank centrality and Gaussian mixture models are combined to reconstruct modules that preserve the structural, spatial and hierarchical properties of the original system — giving an interpretable null model against which real connectomes can be tested.
