Van Life — Chevy Express

Early 2026–present · self-built camper

I bought a Chevy Express and I am converting it into a camper myself — framing, insulation, electrical, cabinetry, and finish — and taking it out on the road. This page is both the build and the life around it: the work, the tools, and where the van has taken me. I do it to learn more hands-on craft, to meet others who make things, and to build toward making more meaningful things over time.

The empty van

I bought a Chevy Express, sold off the shelving and dividers, and vacuumed the whole cargo bay at a car wash until it was back to bare metal. Then washed it at sunset out of a single Home Depot bucket. An empty box — and the start of everything.

Sound deadening

Kilmat on the panels first: the quiet layer you never see once the walls go up, but you hear it on every drive.

The floor

Templated the floor with Amazon boxes and paper bags, cut XPS foam and then a full sheet of plywood with a jigsaw, and test-fit until the edges were right. The moment the floor went down, the box suddenly felt like a room.

A hole in the roof

First time cutting into a roof — a little nervous. A frame from leftover wood, pilot holes, a jigsaw, butyl tape to seal. Not just ventilation: the line where a work truck starts becoming a home.

Wiring, insulation & finish

Sketched the 12V wiring on kraft paper first — planning the runs felt a lot like motion planning. Then Havelock wool over the ceiling and walls, held with nylon rope and 3M spray, a solar panel for power, and the plywood sealed and stained. Every redo was just the search for a better way.

On the road

Then out to Joshua Tree — the reason for all of it. Open desert, a warm glow through the windows at dusk, a chair set up inside. Still a build in progress, already a life on the road.
